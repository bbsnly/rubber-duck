# Sources — rubber-duck

This skill distills two primary sources and the cognitive science beneath them.
The summaries below are paraphrases, not quotations.

- **The Pragmatic Programmer** (Hunt & Thomas) — origin of "rubber ducking":
  explain a problem step by step to a listener who does not need to reply, and
  the act of explaining often makes the problem show itself.
- **Harvard CS50's "duck debugger" (ddb)** (Malan et al.) — an AI rubber duck
  with deliberate pedagogical guardrails: it leads students toward an answer
  instead of handing it over, because general-purpose assistants give answers
  too readily. See https://cs50.readthedocs.io/cs50.ai/ and Liu et al.,
  "Teaching CS50 with AI",
  https://cs.harvard.edu/malan/publications/V1fp0567-liu.pdf
- **Socratic method & self-explanation** — guiding someone to a statement that
  contradicts their own misconception produces the dissonance that drives a
  durable belief update; the act of self-explaining surfaces the gaps in their
  understanding.
